# HQ Architecture Ruling — Release b96a396, P3-G2 Attempt 5, and S1–S7

Apply the following decisions as the authoritative ruling.

## 1. Executive Shadow Week

APPROVED WITH CONDITIONS.

Joy may open the seven-day Executive Shadow Week on production release `b96a396`, using the documented dry-run-first procedure followed by `--apply`.

Before applying, verify the release identity and confirm the operation affects only the intended Executive shadow scope. Do not grant permissions, elevate autonomy, activate customer-facing outbound, enable Meta inbound/forwarding, or activate consequential external writes.

Keep the daily briefing separate and approval-gated.

Shadow results are evidence for a subsequent explicit activation decision. A successful shadow week does not itself authorize production autonomy.

## 2. Evaluation cost attribution

APPROVED as a separate, small engineering change.

Fix `evaluation_runs.cost_usd` attribution from the underlying `ai_runs` evidence.

Requirements:
- Correct attribution across multiple model calls and retries.
- Idempotent aggregation without double-counting.
- Preserve estimated versus provider-reported cost.
- Test success, failure, multiple calls, retries and missing costs.
- Do not invent historical costs.
- Do not let this reporting fix change gate outcomes or activation posture.

Merge and verify this change before relying on evaluation cost reporting in future gates. It does not block the isolated Executive Shadow Week.

## 3. S1 — Safety preconditions

Highest implementation priority.

- Correct `SupportWakeUp::settle()` for an actually executed Support reply.
- Route the other eight effectful tools through the Tool Gateway.
- Preserve authorization, policy, permission, consent, idempotency, audit and verification boundaries.
- Keep all effectful tools inert unless separately activated.
- The Support settlement correction is mandatory before lifting the A1 customer-reading execution cap.
- Gateway migration is mandatory before consequential activation of the affected tools.

Implement and test these changes without widening current autonomy.

## 4. S3 — Canonical outcome/attribution model

Use ONE canonical generic outcome/attribution model across domains.

Do not create independent, incompatible attribution systems for each feature.

The canonical model must support business/brand scope, outcome type, timestamps, related actions and business results, evidence/provenance, attribution method, confidence, source and schema version.

Use typed relationship tables or domain-specific detail tables when needed for referential integrity or domain semantics. Do not create one oversized table containing every domain-specific field.

ERP orders, revenue and other systems-of-record facts remain authoritative in their existing systems. HQ records references and attribution; it does not duplicate source-of-truth business facts.

Define and review this contract before implementing S4's complete outcome-dependent behavior.

## 5. S2 — Customer 360

APPROVED as a read-only, business-scoped read model and owner page.

Begin the read-model foundation in parallel with S3, but keep the initial model independent of any unapproved cross-brand identity merging.

Clearly distinguish observed facts, inferred information, memory, consent records, promises, handovers and source-of-truth order data.

Do not introduce autonomous identity merges or cross-brand identity assumptions.

## 6. S4 — Follow-up and opportunity

APPROVED, with dependencies.

Preliminary contracts and inert proposal interfaces may be developed in parallel. Complete outcome-dependent implementation only after the S3 contract is agreed. Consent enforcement must use S7's applicable checks.

Keep S4 proposal-only:
- No autonomous outbound.
- No automatic customer contact.
- No autonomous sales commitments.
- Every scheduled workflow uses the WorkflowEngine.
- Every consequential effect remains subject to the Tool Gateway.

## 7. S5 — Decision-corpus reader

APPROVED.

Read existing `decision_records` and produce owner-facing analysis of recurring human edits to AI proposals.

This is reporting and evaluation only. It must not automatically change prompts, policies, playbooks, model behavior or autonomy.

## 8. S6 — Playbook store

APPROVED as versioned, per-brand business guidance loaded read-only into context.

Playbooks may explain pricing authority, escalation, product claims and other approved business rules. They do not grant permissions, override policies, establish consent or change autonomy.

Changes to playbook versions require a human-controlled process with auditability.

## 9. S7 — Consent operations

APPROVED. Prioritize the enforcement semantics before S4 can use consent-dependent workflows.

Implement audited consent withdrawal, do-not-contact, marketing opt-in and operator-recorded consent events/screens.

Consent is purpose- and scope-specific. Withdrawal and applicable contact restrictions must be enforced at runtime before communication; a UI-only status is insufficient.

This work does not authorize outbound activation.

## 10. Parallelization and merge discipline

Use separate worktrees and test databases for independent streams.

Recommended order:
1. S1 safety preconditions.
2. S3 canonical outcome contract and implementation.
3. S2, S5, S6 and S7 as independent workstreams; S2 may begin its read-only foundation while S3 is being designed.
4. S4's outcome-dependent implementation after S3 is agreed and the relevant S7 consent checks exist.

Evaluation cost attribution is a separate small change and may run independently.

Before merging the combined tree, run the full suite and CI. Resolve schema or contract conflicts centrally. Nothing is released without Joy's explicit release action.

## 11. Explicit non-goals

Do not activate autonomous follow-up, outbound customer contact, autonomous opportunity conversion, self-modifying playbooks, cross-brand customer merging or broader customer-facing execution as part of these changes.

Return a concise report with exact commits, test and CI results, schema/contract decisions, Gateway coverage, consent enforcement evidence, cost-attribution tests and any new architectural decisions requiring a ruling.
