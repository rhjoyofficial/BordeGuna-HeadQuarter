## What this is, in one paragraph

S4 part 1 (docs/113) made follow-ups and opportunities exist as proposals that a person decides, with consent checked and a workflow run that only waits for a person. It deliberately read and wrote no outcome. With the S3 contract now ruled, this branch connects the two the way docs/107 D7 already foresaw: a follow-up becomes one more kind of action the ONE outcome ledger can credit. Nothing new decides anything. A person who knows that a follow-up led to an order says so through the existing human-only `outcomes.attribute` tool, and HQ shows that credit on the follow-ups page and on Customer 360. An overdue follow-up now tells the person responsible for it, inside HQ, and nobody else. Old refused and declined rows are pruned after a year. Everything that could reach a customer stays absent.

## Built, per ruling item

- **Stage 4, the S3 link (items 1, 3, 5, 6, 7).** New action type `follow_up` (`ActionType::FollowUp`): one typed column `outcome_attributions.follow_up_id`, RESTRICT, and the typed-action CHECK replaced so exactly one of nine columns is set. The recorder refuses to credit a follow-up that is not `done` and, as for every action, one of another business. No new outcome type (item 3: types name business events; "a follow-up was done" is an action). No automatic credit: `afterCompleted` records nothing, because v1 admits no method that links a contact to a later result (`rule_window` is refused, Q3) and a person's word must stay `operator_asserted` (C7). The real binding `App\Outcomes\FollowUps\LedgerFollowUpOutcomeLink` only reads: the current view (D17) of credits, through `visibleTo(viewer)` AND `business_id` = the follow-up's own business on both the attribution and its outcome. An opportunity closed `converted` is labelled "supported by a credited order" or "a person's word only" (docs/113 F4); the close itself is never changed by the ledger. No amount anywhere (D3).
- **Item 14, Customer 360.** `App\Customer360\CustomerFollowUpsReader`: this customer's follow-ups, promises (origin `promise`), opportunities, counts and ledger credits. Every query is bound to the viewer's scope, the customer's id and the customer's business, so a row carrying the id under another business is not shown. Null without `hq.followups.view`. No form, button or route; a snapshot test proves rendering writes nothing. The change to `Customer360Reader` is one line (`follow_ups` replaces the old `promises: recorded false`); the page body lives in its own component (`resources/js/components/customers/follow-ups-section.tsx`).
- **Item 16, overdue alert.** Rule `followups.overdue`, the seventeenth in `hq.alerts.rules`. Overdue = still `scheduled` more than `hq.followups.overdue_after_hours` (24) after the due time (`App\FollowUps\OverdueFollowUps`, one definition for the rule and the badge). One condition per responsible person (the approver, who is also the run's requester), counted only inside that person's current business scope. `AlertCondition` gained an optional `recipientUserId`; the engine tells that person alone (an in-app record naming them, opened, renotified and resolved), never the shared Telegram chat. Their own sidebar badge shows their count. Follow-ups whose approver is inactive, lost the permission or lost the scope form one unaddressed condition on the alerts page. Counts and a date only, no customer data. The rule never moves a follow-up, signals a run or sends anything (snapshot test).
- **Item 13, retention.** `retention.v3`. `FollowUpRetention`: refused and declined follow-ups and declined opportunities are removed by id 365 days after the decision (`followup_refused_keep_days`), follow-ups first. Done, cancelled and closed rows are kept (their applicable rule is relationship history); open rows never go. Keep-clauses win: a follow-up an attribution credits, an opportunity a follow-up still serves. Audit rows stay. The WorkflowEngine rule (terminal and reconciled, 180 days) is the existing one, unchanged; a test proves it prunes a follow-up's finished run and leaves the follow-up intact. One DELETE site, pinned.
- **Item 11, roles.** Deciding needs `hq.followups.decide`, checked first in every deciding method (pinned). brand_manager and support_agent hold view and propose only and are refused even on their own proposal, in the service and on the screen. A decider deciding on their own proposal is allowed by role and recorded: `self_decided: true` in the audit row and "decided by its proposer" on the screen.
- **Items 10 and 12, vocabulary.** `App\FollowUps\FollowUpVocabulary` (`followups.vocabulary.v1`) names every kind, origin, state and close reason. Proposals are built from enums only (pinned by reflection). The database now refuses anything else (CHECKs on origin, state, opportunity kind, state and close reason; a closed opportunity must carry its reason). No `transactional` kind.
- **Item 15, RESTRICT.** A test over `pg_constraint`: every foreign key on and to `follow_ups` and `opportunities`, including the new attribution column, is RESTRICT; a credited follow-up cannot be deleted.
- **Item 17 (and item 8), the structural pin.** The pin that compared the definitions list to a class name is gone. `ListedWorkflowDefinitionsStructureTest` checks every definition production would list (the shipped config file and the live catalogue, which must agree): only sleep and signal steps, no tool key, arguments or retries, and no `Step::tool`, `->execute(`, `ToolGateway` or `App\Tools` in its source or any parent's. A negative control proves the checker catches a tool step, including one in an untaken branch. Item 8: the person-decision signal is sent from one place, `completeFollowUp`, after the decider check.
- **Item 9, consent.** Missing consent refuses; a refused row is final even after consent is recorded (approval is an illegal move; a new proposal is needed); the only ledger call under `App\FollowUps` is `permits()`.

## Deviations and open questions

- **Item 11, read literally.** The brief summarised item 11 as "proposer never approves own proposal". The ruling's words are narrower: brand_manager and support_agent may not approve "merely by creating them", and "an authorized owner/operator makes the decision". Built as ruled: proposing never confers deciding, and an owner or operator deciding on their own proposal is allowed and recorded visibly. A blanket ban would leave a single-operator HQ unable to schedule its own follow-ups. Open: should a self-decision be refused once a second deciding person exists (the item 22 pattern for playbooks)?
- **No automatic outcome on completion.** "A follow-up outcome recorded via the S3 ledger" is met through the ruled path (the human-only tool, now accepting `follow_up`), not by completion writing a ledger row. Completion has no admitted method in v1 and an outcome type for "follow-up done" would name an action (item 3). If an automatic link is wanted later, it is a `rule_window` policy, which needs its own ruling (Q3).
- **Overdue grace of 24 hours** is our choice; the ruling approved the rule, not a number. It is configuration.
- A superseded outcome hides the credits attached to its earlier row (the current view reads current outcomes only). Re-crediting after a correction is a person's act.
