2. FIRST, A RULING NEEDED: A LIVE IDENTITY LEAK.
Defect: IdentityResolver::byIdentity() looks up an identity by channel + external_id + store, never by business. Phone, email, whatsapp, messenger_psid and instagram_igsid identities have store = NULL, so they are looked up group-wide. resolve() and resolveChannelIdentity() use it first; the business-scoped fallback runs only when nothing is found. Result: a Bionic order or message from a phone already known in LaLaDia attaches to the LaLaDia customer. No merge, no merge row, no audit.
Schema: customer_identities has no business_id. Its unique index (channel, external_id, source_store_id) does not constrain null-store rows at all, because PostgreSQL treats NULLs as distinct. So "one row per phone" is app convention only, race-prone, and byIdentity's first() has no ORDER BY. Production is pg17, so NULLS NOT DISTINCT is available.
Second path, identity steal: link() is updateOrCreate on (channel, external_id, store) and sets customer_id to whoever is resolving. A Bionic order whose store-scoped shop_user id belongs to Bionic customer B moves the phone row from LaLaDia customer L to B; the next LaLaDia order then attaches to B. The phone can flip with each order, with no lineage.
What flows wrong (every caller): FactsOrderUpserter (shop webhook, reconcile tool, ERP read-back) sets facts_orders.customer_id to the other business's customer and fills its email, phone, alias, last seen. CustomerRegisteredHandler links a Bionic shop_user to a LaLaDia customer. hq:customers:rebuild re-runs the same resolver and recounts across businesses. ConversationService::ingest opens a Bionic thread whose customer is the LaLaDia customer and records the inbound service consent as (LaLaDia customer, Bionic business). SupportContext puts that customer's orders and order count, with no business filter, into a Bionic Support model context (not live: runtime off). InboxController::ordersFor shows LaLaDia orders on a Bionic thread to the operator. Three more unscoped lookups (ConversationService participant link, linkByIdentity, ThreadIdentity; the last fails closed on ownership). S7 identity-scoped consent assumes an identity belongs to one business; a stolen identity makes that history misleading (resolution fails closed).
Already contained: Customer 360 and the customer list only (docs/117 suppression). The detector finds downstream rows (orders, conversations, consent) whose business differs from the customer's, but cannot flag a null-store identity itself. No test runs the resolver across two businesses (the two-business test builds customers by hand). Production exposure unknown; production has the same resolver.
Options:
A (RECOMMENDED) Business-scoped identity. customer_identities.business_id NOT NULL, backfilled from the owner; customers unique (id, business_id); composite FK (customer_id, business_id); unique (business_id, channel, external_id, source_store_id) NULLS NOT DISTINCT replacing the old index; store must belong to the business. Resolver, link, inbound, ThreadIdentity and linkByIdentity filter by business; SupportContext and the inbox order panel also filter (defence in depth); rebuild per business. Tests: same phone in two businesses = two customers, no cross-business re-point, no duplicate under concurrency, a structural pin that every identity lookup names a business. Existing rows: the backfill is exact; mis-attached orders, conversations, participants and messages are repaired by a "split" step in the quarantine repair (find or create the customer in the row's own business, copy the identity, re-point that business's rows, lineage, audit). Consent events stay as written (append-only); standing restrictions are listed for a person to re-record. Pros: matches your rulings (no cross-brand identity merge, explicit business scope), DB-enforced, closes the null-uniqueness hole and the steal. Cons: one person buying from two brands is two customers (the ruled model); a migration plus a repair.
B Group-level person above per-business customers (persons own identities, customers unique per person + business). Pros: one identity graph, a future group-wide do-not-contact. Cons: stores exactly the cross-business link the rulings forbid, re-keys every reader and S7 identity scope, bigger migration and privacy surface. Can be added above A later.
C Resolver filter only, no migration. Hours of work, but a second row per business works only because of the NULL quirk; no DB guarantee. Acceptable only as step 1 of A.
We recommend A, test-first (C as its first commit), and ask you to rule it release-blocking for waves 1-3, since that release makes identity-scoped consent and Customer 360 visible.
