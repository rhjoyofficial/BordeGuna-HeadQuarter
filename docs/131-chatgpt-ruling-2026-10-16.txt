## 1. Release-blocking identity incident

The cross-business identity defect is a release blocker. Do not release Waves 1–4 until the identity fix, repair readiness, combined candidate CI and recovery rehearsal are verified.

The defect includes both cross-business lookup and identity reassignment. Correcting the UI or resolver alone is insufficient: the database, ingestion, conversation, consent, support and inbox paths must all enforce business ownership.

### 1.1 Containment recommendation

My recommendation is containment Option 2: temporarily stop the HQ scheduler/event-drain execution while preserving all incoming source events as durable, replayable pending events. Alerts and scheduled sweeps will also pause and must be accounted for operationally.

**This production action requires Joy’s explicit decision.** Claude must not stop the production scheduler on its own.

Until Joy decides:
- Continue safe code, test, documentation and isolated-rehearsal work.
- Do not claim the incident is contained.
- Do not release.
- Do not run production repair or mutating backfills.
- Do not open the Executive shadow period.
- Do not replay pending events into the old resolver.

Verify that stopping event processing cannot discard payloads or lose source acknowledgements. If durable replay cannot be proven, stop and report the exact blocker.

### 1.2 Identity design

Keep Wave 4’s Option A design:

- `customer_identities.business_id` is required.
- Customer, store and identity relationships are constrained by composite foreign keys and business ownership.
- Null-store identities use database-enforced uniqueness with the approved PostgreSQL 17 semantics.
- Quarantined rows remain preserved evidence and are excluded from active identity resolution.
- Resolver/link operations require explicit business scope and never fall back to group-wide matching.
- `link()` never silently re-points a customer identity.
- Cross-business conflicts fail closed and produce evidence.

If an identity is quarantined and it is the only matching key, do not create a provisional customer. Keep the source event durably available for review and replay after authorized resolution.

The current `conversations` unique key remains a known limitation. Make it business-scoped with an additive migration in the next wave. Until then, a collision must fail closed and must never attach one business’s conversation to another. Add explicit collision and isolation tests before enabling additional Meta traffic.

## 2. Approvals for all ten Wave 4 questions

Apply these rulings as authoritative.

**1 — Quarantined identity with no alternative key: CONFIRM.** Fail closed, no provisional customer, preserve the original event for replay.

**2 — Global conversation uniqueness: APPROVE additive business scoping in the next wave.** Until then, collision means refusal/retry or quarantine, never cross-business attachment.

**3 — Human identity-conflict resolution: APPROVE.** Build the reviewed conflict screen and human-only audited resolution action in the next wave. No agent may resolve conflicts.

**4 — Follow-up self-decision: APPROVE WITH CONDITION.** A sole eligible owner/operator may decide their own proposal if the result is recorded as `self_decided`. Once another eligible independent decider is available, the proposer must not decide the proposal. Keep all role and business boundaries.

**5 — Follow-up completion and outcomes: CONFIRM.** Completion does not itself write an outcome or attribution. Outcomes are recorded through the separate governed, human-only `outcomes.attribute` capability.

**6 — Overdue grace: CONFIRM.** Keep a configurable 24-hour grace period before issuing the overdue alert. Alert the responsible person inside HQ only. No automatic outreach.

**7 — Corrected outcomes and credits: CONFIRM FOR NOW.** The earlier row’s credits disappear from the current view when superseded. Do not delete historical rows or silently transfer credit. Re-credit requires a person and a new auditable attribution.

**8 — Time-zone backfill: APPROVE WITH A SEPARATE MUTATION GATE.** Dry-run first. Show the affected rows, timestamps, expected correction, business/day grouping and before/after counts. Apply only after Joy has reviewed the evidence and explicitly authorized the mutation. Never use inference to repair an ambiguous timestamp.

**9 — Repair deviations: APPROVE WITH SAFEGUARDS.**
- Every row in the proposed repair batch must be reviewed.
- The batch must contain at least one `needs_repair` row and no disputed rows.
- Preserve standing restrictions through new, correctly scoped, audited restriction records on the target customer. Do not mutate append-only historical consent records or convert a restriction into a grant.
- Preserve a review list for restrictions that require the responsible person to re-record them in the correct scope.
- Keep all business-less memories withheld until a person assigns a business supported by evidence.
- Memory assignment may remain a permissioned, audited human controller action rather than a second Gateway tool, provided it is not agent-callable, cannot bypass business scope, and cannot release a memory with ambiguous ownership.
- Preserve the existing fingerprint, advisory-lock, idempotency, lineage and post-repair verification requirements.

**10 — Non-reversible migration: ACCEPT WITH A HARD RELEASE PRECONDITION.** Once conflict evidence makes the W1 migration’s `down()` refuse, the recovery path is restore from the verified pre-deploy backup using the real `restore.sh`. The VPS Docker rehearsal must prove that script works in an isolated throwaway project. Do not claim rollback is safe merely because `down()` exists.

## 4. Next wave — Phase 3 Customer AI readiness for Meta

Begin Phase 3 readiness engineering only after the Wave 4 release and data-isolation gates pass. Work may proceed on inert code/contracts while waiting for external prerequisites, but do not use live customer channels before all required gates for that channel pass.

### P3-W0 — Meta readiness inventory

Once Meta Business Verification is confirmed, record evidence of:
- verified business and app ownership;
- app mode and app-review status for each permission required;
- owned Facebook Pages, Instagram professional accounts and relevant account IDs;
- WhatsApp Business Account, phone-number/SIM readiness and required number verification;
- system-user credentials and credential kinds;
- webhook subscription and callback readiness;
- approved privacy, consent, data-retention and deletion flows;
- token ownership, rotation/expiry monitoring and revocation response;
- country, billing and commercial prerequisites for each intended channel.

Do not treat Business Verification as equivalent to App Review, production permission approval or WhatsApp number readiness. A missing prerequisite leaves only that channel’s production activation blocked; it does not authorize a workaround or broader permission.

Do not invent approval dates or assume permissions are granted. Capture current provider evidence at the time the external prerequisite becomes available.

### P3-W1 — Finish the customer-channel control plane

Use the existing capability/connector architecture. Do not build a competing messaging platform or bypass HQ’s Tool Gateway.

Before Meta inbound is enabled:
- make conversation identity unique and business-scoped, fixing the known global `external_thread_ref` uniqueness limitation;
- add the approved human-only identity-conflict resolution flow;
- confirm inbound deduplication, event ordering, signature verification, replay protection and durable failure handling;
- ensure channel identity resolution always includes business scope;
- prove that consent is read for the correct identity, business, brand, channel and purpose;
- prove that unresolved or quarantined identities cannot be silently linked or served;
- verify handover state and preserve the existing conservative second-inbound-to-human behavior;
- test operator inbox, Customer 360 and Support context for cross-business isolation.

Do not create a group-level customer identity graph.

### P3-W2 — Channel-specific adapters and credential readiness

Integrate supported Meta channel APIs through thin adapters behind ConnectorRegistry and Tool Gateway.

For Messenger and Instagram, verify each required asset, API permission, token and webhook against the actual approved Meta app/account. For WhatsApp, separately verify the business account, number/SIM, permissions, templates or other channel requirements applicable to the intended message type.

Use test assets/accounts and non-customer messages for integration tests. Test inbound normalization, deduplication, queue retry, outbound provider response, uncertain-send state and reconciliation. No live customer outbound is part of adapter readiness.

Keep tokens in the existing credential vault and ensure provider errors, logs and operator alerts never expose secrets or customer message contents unnecessarily.

### P3-W3 — Inert end-to-end Customer AI tests

Build and execute tests using approved test channels/accounts:
- customer message arrives and is assigned to the correct business and identity;
- consent and identity checks refuse when required evidence is missing;
- AI produces a structured reply proposal without sending it;
- Bengali-script language policy remains enforced for mixed Bangla/English input;
- reply and escalation proposals retain evidence and traceability;
- human handover receives the context it is authorized to see;
- replayed inbound events do not duplicate work or change customer ownership;
- Gateway permission, content-policy refusal and consent refusal remain separate outcomes;
- failed/uncertain sends are not falsely recorded as delivered.

All production write tools must remain inert until their own explicit activation gates. A green integration test does not enable a production channel.

### P3-W4 — Shadow and human-controlled rollout gates

Use a serial activation path, not one broad “Meta ready” switch:

1. integration/readiness test on test assets;
2. inbound shadow on an explicitly approved test or narrowly authorized scope;
3. owner review of identity, grounding, refusals, handovers, costs and failures;
4. human-sent Support proposals, where a person sends after review;
5. channel-specific and capability-specific autonomy review;
6. any later live execution receives a separate explicit authorization.

Do not reactivate n8n forwarding, Meta inbound, customer outbound or Agent A2+ because Business Verification arrives. Each channel and capability has its own credentials, permissions, tests and activation decision.

### P3 completion criteria

Return a channel-by-channel report that identifies:
- the external prerequisites actually granted;
- permissions/app-review evidence;
- assets and credential readiness;
- working integration and reconciliation tests;
- outstanding identity/consent/privacy issues;
- the exact activation gate still required;
- what remains inert.

Phase 3 may be called **readiness complete** only when its stated technical and external prerequisites are evidenced. It must not be called **live** until Joy authorizes the relevant activation.
